# Ozhkaervuneth

> A bronze pomegranate holds the winter that a dragon refused to swallow.

## The Orchard on the Ceiling

The orchard grows above the ceiling of an inn. Its roots descend through the plaster without breaking it, and its fruit can be heard falling whenever a guest forgets why they entered a room. The innkeeper sweeps the floors each morning, gathering only dust and little circles of frost.

There are no stairs to the orchard. A narrow door behind the kitchen opens onto the same kitchen, except that every object casts its shadow toward the stove. The innkeeper keeps this door latched with a spoon made of dark wood.

One fruit hangs beneath the ceiling. It is a pomegranate of bronze, suspended from a root as thin as hair. Its surface remains cold even when the stove is burning. A shallow seam runs around its middle, but nobody has found a way to open it.

## A Guest Without Luggage

The guest arrived carrying a little snow in his closed hand. It did not melt. He asked for a room that faced the orchard, and the innkeeper gave him a room whose window showed the street as it had looked a hundred years earlier.

In that street, a child stood outside the inn holding a wooden spoon. The guest recognized the spoon from behind the kitchen door. He watched the child lift it toward the window, then noticed that the child had no reflection in the puddles below.

At supper, he placed his snow beside the bronze fruit. The snow unfolded into a white leaf. Along its veins ran a series of tiny rooms, each containing an unlit stove and an empty chair.

The innkeeper covered the leaf with a plate. The pomegranate turned slowly on its root. From above the ceiling came the sound of somebody walking through fallen fruit without crushing it.

## The Table Beneath the Root

They moved the supper table beneath the pomegranate. Its bronze surface reflected the room, but the reflection contained one additional person: a woman seated beside the guest, holding the white leaf against her chest.

The guest remembered her coat. He remembered buttons carved from pale horn and a sleeve that smelled of apples. He could not remember her face, although he had spent much of his life expecting to recognize it in a crowd.

The innkeeper set a third place at the table. Its spoon filled with frost. In the bronze reflection, the woman turned toward the kitchen door and laid one finger across her lips.

Above them, a root thickened. Its bark acquired the texture of scales. The falling fruit became silent, and the stove's flame bent toward the ceiling as though drawn by a breath too large for the room.

## What the Orchard Grew Around

The innkeeper had once entered the upper orchard in a dream. There were no trees there. The roots belonged to a creature lying on its back beneath a sky of dark soil. Its horns held the branches apart. Its wings were folded around an empty space shaped like a house.

Fruit grew along its ribs. Some were glass, some were stone, and some resembled small suns with their light extinguished. The bronze pomegranate hung nearest its throat, where a narrow patch of frost interrupted the scales.

The creature had offered the innkeeper a seed. She had brought it back beneath her tongue and planted it in the stove. Since that night, the fire had never needed kindling, and every guest had dreamed of a room just beyond their own.

She told the guest none of this until the third spoon began to move.

## The Words Inside the Seam

The pomegranate opened by the width of a thread. Along its inner edge were marks that could be seen only in the reflection of the frosted spoon:

*Ozh — kaer — vuneth — iluun.*

The guest spoke them while holding the white leaf. The seam widened. Within the bronze shell lay a field of snow, and across the field ran a line of small footprints leading toward a house without windows.

The woman in the reflection stood. Her chair remained beside the table, though her place in the bronze surface was empty. Behind the kitchen door, something tapped three times with the handle of a wooden spoon.

The innkeeper removed the latch. The second kitchen was gone. Beyond the threshold stood the child from the old street, now holding the woman's hand. Snow fell around them without entering the room.

## The Room That Was Finally Warm

The guest stepped toward the threshold. The white leaf became warm in his hand, and the little rooms along its veins filled with orange light. For one moment, he remembered every face he had searched for and every room in which he had waited.

He placed the leaf inside the pomegranate. The bronze shell closed gently around it. Above the ceiling, the creature shifted its wings, and the inn's walls settled as though a great weight had been lifted from their foundations.

By morning, the guest's room stood empty. Its window showed the ordinary street. On the bed lay a wooden spoon, polished smooth by years of use.

The innkeeper still sets three places beneath the root on the coldest evenings.

The bronze fruit never opens.

But frost no longer gathers beneath it, and the orchard overhead has learned to fall without waking anyone.
